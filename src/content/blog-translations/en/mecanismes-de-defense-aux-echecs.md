Five players lose a game. One blames noise, another jokes, a third wants to analyse immediately. Those reactions invite questions. They are not enough to place five people in a hierarchy of maturity.

The vocabulary of defence mechanisms can help you reflect on experiencing defeat. It becomes much less useful when every behaviour serves to diagnose the opponent.

## A defence in what sense?

In this psychological framework, a defence means a way of managing a conflict or difficult feeling, sometimes without awareness. The idea is not equivalent to a deliberate lie. But you also cannot conclude that an explanation is unconscious merely because it annoys you.

Noise or an incorrectly set clock can genuinely be a problem. Before treating it as an excuse, distinguish a verifiable fact from an interpretation.

## What Vaillant and colleagues studied

Vaillant, Bond and Vaillant (1986) examine the defences of 307 men followed for forty years. Assessments based on interviews at age 47 correlate with independent measures of mental health. A questionnaire later administered to part of the group finds comparable styles.

This supports the value of studying defensive styles. It does not validate instantly classifying players from their exit from a room, or show that a particular gesture has an “identifiable neurology”. The study does not concern chess.

Maturity here belongs to a psychological assessment framework. It measures neither a person’s worth nor chess strength.

## Examples for examining your own account

[Rationalisation](/en/blog/rationalization-in-chess/) can be a possibility to examine when you construct a later justification that does not match your thoughts during the game. Check it by comparing your account with your memories and the variations; a feeling of clarity is not enough.

[Projection](/en/blog/projection-in-chess/) invites you to examine what you attribute to someone else. If you ascribe contempt without an interaction or clear evidence, you may mainly have a hypothesis about their intentions.

Intellectualisation is another possible term when technical explanation avoids any contact with disappointment. Yet analysing a variation may also be a pleasure or a legitimate need. Visible behaviour does not, by itself, determine its function.

Humour can help create distance, but may also hurt or conceal a difficulty. It is not automatically evidence of greater maturity.

## Returning to facts after defeat

You can try a brief written review after the game: what happened, what you felt and what you conclude. Separate those three elements. “I lost after missing an intermediate check” is a technical observation; “I can never calculate” is a generalisation.

If you are still too agitated to analyse, postpone the exercise. One hour or twenty-four hours is not a universal prescription: choose a moment when you can examine the game without restarting the conflict.

## Using the framework without confining people

You can ask a coach to challenge your explanation with a variation. That is more useful than a personality label. If analysing with a friend, start with what they want to understand rather than what you think you have exposed.

This framework remains an invitation to examine your account. It turns neither a game notebook into a diagnostic tool nor a post-tournament routine into psychological treatment.
